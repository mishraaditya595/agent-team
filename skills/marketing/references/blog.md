# Blog post

Adapted from coreyhaines31/marketingskills `content-strategy` and `copywriting` (MIT).

## Decide the job first

Every post is **searchable** (answers a question people already search for), **shareable** (a story, an insight or an opinion people pass on), or both. Say which at the top of the draft.

- **Searchable:** pick one specific question a user of this product would type. Write one sentence on what this post adds that the usual answers don't — a real example from the product, a working template, a clearer answer, a firm opinion. If you can't write that sentence, pick another angle. Put the question's words in the title, the first paragraph and at least one heading; answer it directly in the first 100 words.
- **Shareable:** lead with the insight, the result or the story — how it was built, what was learned, what went wrong. Concrete beats clever.

## Write it

1. **Ten title options**, then pick the strongest and say why. The title does most of the work.
2. Open with the reader's problem in their words, not the product.
3. One idea per section; headings a skimmer could read alone (they double as social posts later).
4. Show, don't claim: screenshots, the actual steps, a real example from the product.
5. The product appears where it genuinely solves the problem in the post, not as an ad in every section.
6. End with one next step (try it, read the related post, join the list).
7. 800–1,500 words unless the topic really needs more.

## SEO basics

- **Meta description:** ≤ 155 characters, the promise of the post in plain words.
- **Slug:** short, lowercase, the main words of the question.
- Suggest 2–3 internal links (existing pages or posts in the product's site, if you know them) and one useful outside source.

## Output

```
Job: searchable (<query>) | shareable · Adds: <one sentence>
Title options: 1–10 · Chosen: <n> — <why>
Slug: <slug> · Meta description: <≤155 chars>
---
<the full draft in markdown>
---
Pull-outs for social: <2–3 lines from the post that work alone>
```
